Radio · operator reference

What the meter is telling you, and when MP3 goes missing

Two things on the station page get asked about, and both have precise answers. The waveform is the audio we are broadcasting, measured on the server, not the audio your laptop is playing. The bar under it is a peak hold over the visible window, not the level right now. And a degraded MP3 output means one specific thing, which is exactly what DEF CON Radio is reporting: the station asked for an MP3 output, the relay it was told to push to is not running, and it went on air over HLS and DASH without it.

The waveform

Switch the signal below to see each state the station can report.

Signal
The barsServer-measured true peak

Each bar is one ~100 ms frame of true peak level, measured by ffmpeg ebur128=peak=true on the newest complete segment the packager wrote. 120 bars are kept, drained at their real cadence, so the visible window is about 12 seconds and the motion is the stream's own, not an animation.

The fadeLeft is older

Opacity ramps from 0.35 on the left to full on the right, so the newest audio is the brightest. Bars scroll leftwards and fall off. A flat grey row means measured silence rather than no measurement.

The heightLinear amplitude, floored at -60 dBFS

Not loudness and not dB mapped linearly. Broadcast audio is loudness-normalised, so a loudness scale renders every bar near the top and shows no dynamics at all. Peak amplitude is what a waveform view actually draws.

The colourThe verdict, not the level

Every bar takes the colour of the current verdict, so the whole waveform turns amber the moment the station is classified as low, and red on dead air. The height still says how loud; the colour says what kind of signal it is.

The bar underneathPeak hold over the window

This is the one people read wrong. Its width is the tallest bar currently on screen, not the level at this instant. It only falls when the loudest moment of the last 12 seconds scrolls off the left, so it lags on purpose and answers "has this station peaked recently", not "how loud is it right now".

The badgeWhat kind of signal it is

Top left, and it is a classification rather than a measurement: programme audio, low, dead air, or noise-like. It appears only for a live station, because a finished recording has no server feed to classify.

The line belowThe newest segment's numbers

peak is the loudest sample in it, rms the average energy, and crest is the gap between them. updated Ns ago is the age of that measurement, so a number that climbs past about ten seconds means the monitor has stopped getting segments, which is a fault the waveform itself will not show yet.

Crest factor is the one worth knowing

Peak minus RMS. Speech and music are peaky, so they run wide: your DEF CON Radio station measured 15.1 dB and 12.3 dB in the two screenshots, which is healthy programme audio. Noise is flat, so hiss, a stuck tone or a dead carrier collapses toward a few dB. That is why the static test is crest under 8 dB together with high spectral entropy, and it is the check that catches a station that is technically receiving something but is not receiving the show.

One sentence for a client

"That is the audio coming out of the platform, measured on our servers every tenth of a second, over the last twelve seconds. The bar underneath is the loudest point in that window."

Why a loud station is not a full-height waveform

The height scale, in numbers, because this one surprises people.

True peakBar heightReads as
-1 dBFS89%a transient, near clipping
-4 dBFS63%loud music peak
-13 dBFS22%normal programme
-20 dBFS10%quiet passage, speech tail
-40 dBFS1%room tone
-60 dBFS0%floored to silence

So the waveform looks "low" and that is correct

A properly mastered station sitting around -13 dBFS peak draws bars about a fifth of the height, with transients spiking well above. That is a healthy picture, not a quiet one.

The alternative was tried and discarded. EBU R128 momentary loudness moved under 4 LU across a real music segment, and a 60 dB linear window put -4 dBFS at 0.93 and -13 dBFS at 0.78. Both produce a solid block pinned near the top with no dynamics visible at all.

Loudness is still measured and kept, it is simply not what draws the picture.

What the waveform is not

Four things it deliberately does not tell you

  • It is not your playback. Pressing Listen, muting, or stopping changes nothing on screen except the mute icon. There used to be a second blue overlay fed from the browser, and it was removed because two waveforms in one box invite the reading "the picture changes when I press play".
  • It is not the source. It is measured on what the packager wrote, which is what listeners receive. A healthy source and a broken output look different here, on purpose.
  • It is not loudness. Height is peak amplitude. Do not read a compliance number off it.
  • It is not live to the millisecond. It measures the newest complete segment, so it trails the live edge by roughly one segment.

The one exception

On the recording workbench there is no server feed, because the thing being visualised is a finished file. There the same waveform is fed from the browser's own audio analyser, using peak amplitude on the same scale so it draws the same picture. That is the only place local playback moves the bars, and the verdict badge is hidden there rather than reporting "no measurement" about a completed capture.

Degraded MP3

One specific failure, with one specific cause.

The short version

"Degraded" means the running encoder refused part of what it was asked to produce and went on air with the rest. Only MP3 can do this, and only in the topology where the station pushes MP3 to an Icecast relay, because MP3 is the only output that needs a second piece of infrastructure to exist.

Why only MP3

HLS and DASH are files this process writes to the origin. If the process is running, they exist. MP3 in relay mode is a live push to another server, so it can be missing while the station is otherwise perfectly healthy, for a reason that has nothing to do with the source.

Why it is not just an error

One ffmpeg carries every output, and ffmpeg opens all of its outputs before it encodes anything. A refused icecast:// connection does not cost you the MP3, it kills the process. The station then produces nothing at all, exits about a second after starting, and auto-connect restarts it twenty seconds later, forever. That is not hypothetical: it filled a demo machine with ninety run directories.

What actually happens, end to end

1
Preflight, before ffmpeg is built

The station opens a plain TCP connection to the relay's host and port, three second timeout. Deliberately shallow: it does not authenticate and does not check the mount, because the only question is whether it is worth including this output at all. A relay that accepts and then rejects credentials is a real error and should still fail loudly.

2
If nothing is listening, MP3 is dropped from this run

Removed from the config before the argument builder ever sees it, so the builder stays a pure function and keeps parity with its TypeScript twin. The station then goes on air over HLS and DASH. If MP3 was the only output configured it does not degrade, it dies, because a station with nothing to deliver must not claim to be on air.

3
It says so, with the address

An output.degraded event carrying no Icecast relay listening at host:port. The address is the whole diagnosis, and by the time anyone reads the event the container is usually gone.

4
The control plane records it against the run

Stored on stream_status.degraded_outputs as {output: reason}, and cleared on the next start, because a new run makes its own decisions and must not inherit the last one's.

5
The API stops advertising the URL

A degraded output returns null instead of a URL, plus the reason. A URL offered by this API is a promise that there is audio behind it.

6
The console says which and why

The output row shows the reason inline, and if you press Listen with nothing left to attach to, the banner reads "No output this player can use" followed by the reason.

The bug this was built to kill

Before any of the above existed, the encoder already dropped MP3 and reported it, and nothing consumed the event. So the API went on advertising an MP3 URL for an output that did not exist, the console tried MP3 first, and the failed play was swallowed. On a machine with no Icecast the result was a station visibly on air, producing perfectly good HLS, playing nothing when you pressed Listen, and reporting no error anywhere. It was a reporting bug, not an audio one.

Which is exactly what DEF CON Radio is showing you

Three topologies, and MP3 means something different in each.

Your station, read back

The header says ICECAST in · 128k out and the MP3 row says unavailable — no Icecast relay listening at localhost:8000. So this station has its MP3 output explicitly on with a relay configured at localhost:8000, and nothing is running there. The preflight found that before ffmpeg started, dropped MP3 from the run, and put the station on air over HLS and DASH, which are both serving.

Everything else on that page is correct and healthy. Server, input and encoder are all green, the signal is programme audio at -3.3 dBFS peak with 12.3 dB of crest, and it is recording. One output of three is missing, for a reason that has nothing to do with the audio.

Start Icecast on port 8000 and restart the station and the MP3 row fills in, because the degraded record is cleared on start and the next run runs its own preflight. Or turn the station's MP3 output off and let the on-demand gateway derive MP3 from the HLS instead, which is the default for new stations and needs no relay at all.

And it explains the zero beside it

The now-playing line reads 0 MP3 listeners. With MP3 degraded that number can only ever be zero, because there is no MP3 output for anyone to be listening to. Read on its own it looks like an audience problem. It is the same fault, counted from the other end.

TopologyWhat MP3 meansCan it degrade?
Container, no relay
today's default		 The station writes HLS and knows nothing about MP3. A listener's request starts one ffmpeg that reads the origin and transcodes, stopping shortly after the last listener leaves. No. There is no relay to probe, so the preflight never runs.
Container plus relay The station pushes MP3 to Icecast. The public URL points at the relay and the control plane redirects to it. Yes. This is the only case that produces a degraded MP3.
Legacy in-process The control plane spawns the station's ffmpeg and reads MP3 off its output, so the control plane holds the bytes and serves them itself. No relay involved, so no preflight.

Which is why a newer station will never show this

Stations now default to mp3: false with HLS and DASH on, and MP3 derived on demand. A degraded MP3 requires the output explicitly on, pointed at a relay, with that relay not running, which is the older shape your DEF CON Radio station is still configured in. So two stations side by side can behave differently for a reason that is in their config rather than in the platform, and this is the row that tells them apart.

The reason the flag alone is not the discriminator: outputs.mp3 means three different things across those rows, and keying on it alone once read the legacy case as the relay case and sent listeners to a mount nothing was pushing to.

The meter above is a faithful replica of that drawing code, running on synthetic audio.
